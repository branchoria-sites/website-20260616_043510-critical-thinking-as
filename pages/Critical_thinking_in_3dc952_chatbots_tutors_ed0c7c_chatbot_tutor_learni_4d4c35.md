## Introduction

Chatbot tutors help students learn best when they act like good tutors rather than efficient answer machines. The central lesson emerging from recent research is surprisingly consistent: learning improves when AI asks questions, provides feedback, adapts explanations and encourages students to work through problems themselves. When students use a chatbot mainly to obtain completed answers, the benefits often shrink or disappear. In some cases, [overreliance]({{ 'overreliance/' | relative_url }}) can even reduce learning because students engage less deeply with the material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/what-the-research-shows-about-generative-ai-in-tutoring/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: brookings.edu">[Brookings]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">brookings.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">what the research shows about generative ai in tutoring</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">What the research shows about generative AI in tutoringJan 27, 2026 — Mary Burns unpacks the evidence of generative AI in tutori...</span></span></span>

This distinction matters for [critical thinking]({{ 'critical-thinking-in-the-age-of-social/' | relative_url }}) in the age of social media and AI. A student who receives an instant solution may finish an assignment more quickly, but a student who must explain reasoning, evaluate [evidence]({{ 'evidence/' | relative_url }}) and revise mistakes is more likely to develop durable understanding. The most promising AI tutoring systems are therefore designed around pedagogy—how people learn—not simply around information delivery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.khanmigo.ai/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: khanmigo.ai">[khanmigo.ai]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">khanmigo.ai</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">e on prep, tackle homework challenges, and get personalized tutoring...</span></span></span>

## What Custom AI Tutoring Trials Suggest

The strongest evidence for chatbot tutoring comes from systems that deliberately structure interactions around learning principles rather than unrestricted conversation.

A widely discussed 2025 study published in *Scientific Reports* compared a custom-built AI tutor with in-class active-learning instruction in a university physics course. Students using the AI tutor learned significantly more in less time, while also reporting greater engagement and motivation. Crucially, the tutor was not merely answering questions. Its design incorporated instructional techniques similar to those used by experienced educators, including guided explanations and interactive problem solving.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-97652-6" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[Nature+2PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AI tutoring outperforms in-class active learningby G Kestin · 2025 · Cited by 147 — We find that students learn significantly more...</span></span></span>

Other educational projects have reached a similar conclusion. Khan Academy's Khanmigo was intentionally designed to avoid simply handing over solutions. Instead, it prompts students with questions and hints that help them arrive at answers themselves. The underlying assumption is that productive struggle—the effort required to think through a problem—is part of learning and should not be removed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.khanmigo.ai/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: khanmigo.ai">[khanmigo.ai]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">khanmigo.ai</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">e on prep, tackle homework challenges, and get personalized tutoring...</span></span></span>

Research reviews published in 2026 suggest that generative-AI tutoring systems can perform many functions traditionally associated with human tutoring, including personalised explanations, feedback and adaptive support. However, the most successful implementations tend to be carefully constrained educational tools rather than open-ended chatbots operating without pedagogical structure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/what-the-research-shows-about-generative-ai-in-tutoring/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: brookings.edu">[Brookings]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">brookings.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">what the research shows about generative ai in tutoring</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">What the research shows about generative AI in tutoringJan 27, 2026 — Mary Burns unpacks the evidence of generative AI in tutori...</span></span></span>

At the same time, evidence remains mixed. Some studies have found that students with access to generative-[AI tutors]({{ 'ai-tutors/' | relative_url }}) perform better on practice activities yet fail to achieve corresponding gains on later assessments because they rely on the system too heavily. A large study of secondary-school mathematics students found that access to AI assistance improved performance during practice but often encouraged answer-copying behaviours that weakened subsequent learning.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.axios.com/local/san-francisco/2024/08/22/ai-tutor-bay-area-classrooms" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: axios.com">[Axios]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">axios.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The study, involving nearly 1,000 students from grades 9 to 11 over four 90-minute sessions, found that while genAI can help with practic...</span></span></span>

The implication is not that chatbot tutoring fails. Rather, outcomes depend heavily on how the tutor is designed and how students are encouraged to use it.

## Why Human-in-the-Loop Support Matters

One of the clearest findings across recent research is that human support still matters, especially for struggling learners.

Studies of hybrid human-AI tutoring programmes have repeatedly found advantages over AI-only approaches. Researchers examining tutoring programmes in middle schools observed improvements in student engagement, usage and proficiency when human tutors worked alongside AI systems. Benefits appeared particularly strong for lower-achieving students who often need motivational support, encouragement and intervention when they become stuck.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11274" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Improving Student Learning with Hybrid Human-AI Tutoring: A Three-Study Quasi-Experimental InvestigationDecember 18, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 18, 2023</span></span></span>

More recent work suggests that the most effective arrangement may be differentiated support rather than constant human supervision. In one large study involving more than 600 students, combining AI tutoring with targeted human assistance increased time on task, skill proficiency and academic growth. Students who needed more support benefited from proactive human intervention, while stronger students often succeeded with more independent AI-guided learning.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11155" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Improving Hybrid Human-AI Tutoring by Differentiating Human Tutor Roles Based on Student NeedsMay 11, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 11, 2026</span></span></span>

This reflects a broader reality about education. Tutors do more than explain content. They notice frustration, encourage persistence, identify misconceptions and adapt instruction to emotional as well as academic needs. Current AI systems can imitate some of these behaviours but often struggle to match the flexibility and judgement of experienced educators.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.01914" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">How Real Is AI Tutoring? For schools and universities, the practical lesson is that AI tutoring should often be viewed as a complement to teachers rather than a replacement. The most effective implementations use AI to extend access to practice and feedback while reserving human attention for the moments where human judgement matters most.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/what-the-research-shows-about-generative-ai-in-tutoring/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: brookings.edu">[Brookings]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">brookings.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">what the research shows about generative ai in tutoring</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">What the research shows about generative AI in tutoringJan 27, 2026 — Mary Burns unpacks the evidence of generative AI in tutori...</span></span></span>

## Practice Designs That Preserve Student Effort

The difference between helpful tutoring and harmful shortcutting often comes down to instructional design.

Several design choices appear especially important:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--checklist" markdown="1">

* **Socratic questioning:** Instead of revealing answers immediately, the chatbot asks students to explain their reasoning or consider alternatives.
* **Hints before solutions:** Students receive progressively stronger guidance rather than instant completion.
* **Explanation prompts:** Learners must describe how they reached an answer.
* **Error-focused feedback:** The tutor identifies misconceptions and encourages revision.
* **Reflection activities:** Students compare approaches, evaluate evidence or justify conclusions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.khanmigo.ai/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: khanmigo.ai">[khanmigo.ai+2Wordpress Multisite]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">khanmigo.ai</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">e on prep, tackle homework challenges, and get personalized tutoring...</span></span></span>

</div>

These approaches preserve what educational researchers often call cognitive effort. Learning generally requires mental work. If a chatbot removes too much of that work, students may complete tasks successfully without developing understanding.

This concern is reflected in emerging research on AI tutor evaluation. Researchers analysing more than 10,000 student submissions argued that judging tutor quality solely by the apparent quality of feedback is insufficient. What matters is whether students actually engage with the feedback and use it correctly. An AI tutor can produce impressive explanations yet still fail educationally if students simply accept its output without thinking.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05648" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Missing Evaluation Axis: What 10,000 Student Submissions Reveal About AI Tutor EffectivenessMay 7, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 7, 2026</span></span></span>

From a critical-thinking perspective, this is a crucial distinction. The goal is not merely to receive information but to practise reasoning. Chatbot tutors contribute most when they require learners to make decisions, test ideas and revise beliefs rather than passively consume answers.

## The Critical-Thinking Advantage

The strongest case for chatbot tutors is not that they know more than teachers or textbooks. It is that they can provide immediate, personalised interaction at a scale that traditional education often cannot.

A student can ask the same question five different ways, request a simpler explanation, generate additional practice problems or receive instant feedback at any hour. When those interactions are structured around questioning, explanation and revision, they can create many more opportunities for active learning than students would otherwise receive.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-97652-6" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[Nature+2Brookings]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AI tutoring outperforms in-class active learningby G Kestin · 2025 · Cited by 147 — We find that students learn significantly more...</span></span></span>
