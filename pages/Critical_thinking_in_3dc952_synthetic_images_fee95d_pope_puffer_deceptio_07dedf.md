## Introduction

The image of Pope Francis wearing a dramatic white puffer jacket became one of the defining AI-generated pictures of 2023. It was not a political hoax, a financial scam or a fabricated war photograph. In many ways, it was simply a joke. Yet that is precisely why it mattered.

The “Pope puffer” image showed how easily people can accept a synthetic picture when it feels plausible, entertaining and socially shareable. Millions encountered the image before learning it had been generated with Midjourney, and many assumed it was authentic. The episode became an early warning that visual realism alone was no longer a reliable guide to truth. More importantly, it demonstrated how harmless-looking fakes can reshape everyday habits of trust long before they are used for more serious deception.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.reuters.com/article/fact-check/image-of-pope-francis-wearing-oversized-white-puffer-coat-is-ai-generated-idUSL1N36120G/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: reuters.com">[Reuters+2CBS News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">reuters.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Image of Pope Francis wearing oversized white puffer coat...Mar 29, 2023 — An image showing Pope Francis wearing a white-colored...</span></span></span>

## Why the image felt believable enough to share

When the image appeared online in March 2023, many viewers did not stop to investigate its origin. The picture seemed realistic enough to fit naturally into a social-media feed. Reuters, Full Fact and other fact-checking organisations later confirmed that the image was AI-generated rather than a genuine photograph.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.reuters.com/article/fact-check/image-of-pope-francis-wearing-oversized-white-puffer-coat-is-ai-generated-idUSL1N36120G/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: reuters.com">[Reuters]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">reuters.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Image of Pope Francis wearing oversized white puffer coat...Mar 29, 2023 — An image showing Pope Francis wearing a white-colored...</span></span></span>

Several factors made it unusually convincing:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--example" markdown="1">

* **The subject was instantly recognisable.** Pope Francis is one of the world's most photographed public figures.
* **The clothing was surprising but not impossible.** The oversized white coat looked extravagant, yet it still resembled luxury fashion that celebrities and public figures sometimes wear.
* **The image quality was high.** Lighting, fabric texture and facial details appeared photographically coherent.
* **The scenario was low stakes.** Viewers were not evaluating a serious claim about crime, conflict or public safety. They were simply reacting to an amusing image.

</div>

Because the picture did not demand urgent action or provoke fear, many people treated it as entertainment rather than information requiring verification. That reduced the motivation to ask basic questions such as where the image came from or whether a reputable photographer had published it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.poynter.org/fact-checking/2023/pope-francis-balenciaga-puffer-jacket-artificial-intelligence-fact-check/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: poynter.org">[Poynter]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">poynter.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">pope francis balenciaga puffer jacket artificial intelligence fact check</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">It was actually created with the AI image...Read more...</span></span></span>

## How humour lowers verification pressure

Many discussions of misinformation focus on deliberate attempts to deceive. The [Pope puffer image]({{ 'pope-puffer-6a4060/' | relative_url }}) illustrates a different dynamic: people often lower their guard when content is funny.

Humorous content benefits from a social norm that encourages rapid sharing. A person reposting a joke is rarely expected to perform rigorous fact-checking first. In practice, the audience often evaluates whether something is amusing before evaluating whether it is authentic.

This creates a subtle pathway for casual deception:

1. A synthetic image appears amusing.
2. Users share it because it is entertaining.
3. The image gains visibility and credibility through [repetition]({{ 'repetition/' | relative_url }}).
4. Some viewers encounter it detached from its original context.
5. Authenticity becomes harder to assess because popularity itself acts as a credibility signal.

The Pope image spread partly because it generated delight rather than [outrage]({{ 'outrage/' | relative_url }}). Commentators, celebrities and ordinary users admitted that they initially assumed it was real. A widely discussed online poll cited by fact-checking journalists found that many respondents either believed the image or were unsure whether it was genuine.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.poynter.org/fact-checking/2023/pope-francis-balenciaga-puffer-jacket-artificial-intelligence-fact-check/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: poynter.org">[Poynter]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">poynter.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">pope francis balenciaga puffer jacket artificial intelligence fact check</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">It was actually created with the AI image...Read more...</span></span></span>

This is an important lesson for [critical thinking]({{ 'critical-thinking-in-the-age-of-social/' | relative_url }}). People often associate deception with malicious intent, but a misleading image can shape beliefs even when the creator's goal is humour. The consequences arise not only from intent but from how audiences process and distribute the content.

## What casual fakes teach about visual trust

The Pope puffer episode exposed a weakness in a common online habit: judging authenticity primarily by appearance.

For years, internet users were advised to spot manipulated images by looking for obvious flaws—distorted hands, strange shadows or impossible backgrounds. The Pope image demonstrated that a synthetic picture could succeed even when viewers knew such advice. Many people simply never reached the stage of close inspection because the image already felt believable at a glance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/news/will-ai-generated-images-create-new-crisis-fact-checkers-experts-are-not-so-sure" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk">[reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">will ai generated images create new crisis fact checkers experts are not so sure</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Will AI-generated images create a new crisis for fact-...11 Apr 2023 — The rise of AI generated images is indeed a growing concern since...</span></span></span>

The deeper lesson was not that AI had become perfect. Careful observers eventually identified visual inconsistencies, and [fact-checkers]({{ 'fact-checkers/' | relative_url }}) debunked the image quickly. The important point was that most social-media consumption is not careful observation. It is fast, distracted and contextual. People scroll rather than investigate.

The image therefore highlighted a shift in the burden of proof:

* In the past, a realistic photograph often received the benefit of the doubt.
* Increasingly, realism alone is insufficient [evidence]({{ 'evidence/' | relative_url }}).
* Source, provenance and independent confirmation matter more than visual quality.

Researchers, journalists and media-literacy experts have pointed to the Pope image as an early cultural milestone because it showed ordinary users encountering photorealistic AI outside specialist technology circles. It transformed a technical discussion about generative models into a practical demonstration visible to millions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/news/will-ai-generated-images-create-new-crisis-fact-checkers-experts-are-not-so-sure" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk">[reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk+2Vox]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">will ai generated images create new crisis fact checkers experts are not so sure</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Will AI-generated images create a new crisis for fact-...11 Apr 2023 — The rise of AI generated images is indeed a growing concern since...</span></span></span>

## A small deception with a larger warning

The lasting significance of the Pope puffer image is not that many people briefly believed the Pope owned a fashionable coat. The factual error itself was trivial. What mattered was the demonstration effect.

The image proved that an invented scene could achieve the visual authority traditionally associated with photography. Sometimes they are the ones that feel harmless enough to bypass scepticism.
