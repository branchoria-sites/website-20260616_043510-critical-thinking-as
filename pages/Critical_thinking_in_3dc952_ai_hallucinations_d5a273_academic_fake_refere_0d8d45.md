## Introduction

Hallucinated [academic references]({{ 'academic-references/' | relative_url }}) are among the most damaging forms of AI-generated error because they attack one of research’s core trust mechanisms: the citation. Scholars do not merely cite sources to decorate an argument. References provide an evidence trail that allows readers to verify claims, trace ideas, assess credibility and assign credit. When an AI system invents articles, authors, journal issues or publication details that look genuine, it creates a false appearance of scholarly support. The result is more than a simple mistake. Fake references can mislead readers, waste research time, distort credit allocation and contaminate the scientific record as erroneous citations spread from one paper to another. Studies of AI-generated literature reviews and reference lists have repeatedly found substantial rates of fabricated or inaccurate citations, making reference verification an increasingly important part of [critical thinking]({{ 'critical-thinking-in-the-age-of-social/' | relative_url }}) in the age of AI.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-41032-5" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[Nature+2JMIR Mental Health]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fabrication and errors in the bibliographic citations...by WH Walters · 2023 · Cited by 529 — This study investigates one particul...</span></span></span>

## How Generated References Imitate Scholarly Form

Hallucinated references are often difficult to spot because they mimic the structure of legitimate academic citations. Instead of producing obviously nonsensical entries, AI systems frequently combine real author names, plausible article titles, existing journals and realistic publication dates into references that appear authentic at first glance. Researchers examining AI-generated citations have found that many fabricated references are topically relevant and follow standard citation formats even though the underlying publication does not exist.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-41032-5" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[Nature+2Taylor &amp; Francis Online]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fabrication and errors in the bibliographic citations...by WH Walters · 2023 · Cited by 529 — This study investigates one particul...</span></span></span>

This imitation of scholarly form creates a specific credibility problem. Readers are trained to treat citations as signals that [evidence]({{ 'evidence/' | relative_url }}) exists somewhere beyond the text. When a citation looks correct, many people assume that another scholar, reviewer or editor has already verified it. AI systems exploit that expectation unintentionally. They generate references that satisfy the visual and structural conventions of scholarship without providing the underlying evidence those conventions are meant to represent.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12826005/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fabricated citations in the age of AI: A wake-up call for editors...by H Liu · 2025 · Cited by 1 — Large language models (LLMs), such...</span></span></span>

The danger is amplified because fabricated citations often contain partial truths. A journal may be real while the article is invented. An author may exist while the title is fabricated. A genuine paper may be cited but assigned the wrong findings. These hybrid errors are harder to detect than completely fictional references because they reward superficial checking while defeating deeper verification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-41032-5" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[Nature]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fabrication and errors in the bibliographic citations...by WH Walters · 2023 · Cited by 529 — This study investigates one particul...</span></span></span>

## What Citation Studies Found in AI-Written Text

Empirical research has shown that citation fabrication is not a rare edge case. One widely cited study evaluating GPT-generated references found substantial rates of fabricated bibliographic citations, with many references corresponding to no actual scholarly work. Even when citations referred to real publications, metadata errors remained common.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-41032-5" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[Nature]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fabrication and errors in the bibliographic citations...by WH Walters · 2023 · Cited by 529 — This study investigates one particul...</span></span></span>

More recent studies have reached similar conclusions. Research examining GPT-4o-generated literature reviews in mental health topics reported extensive citation inaccuracies, including fabricated references and bibliographic errors. Other analyses focused specifically on scientific writing have found that citation generation remains one of the weakest areas of large language model performance. Reviews of AI-produced academic references consistently report that accuracy rates vary by topic, prompting style and model version, but fabrication remains a recurring problem across systems.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.jmir.org/2024/1/e53164/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: jmir.org">[JMIR+2JMIR Medical Education]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">jmir.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Hallucination Rates and Reference Accuracy of ChatGPT...by M Chelli · 2024 · Cited by 452 — The aim of the study is to assess the pe...</span></span></span>

## Why Fake References Damage Research More Than Ordinary Errors

A factual mistake inside a paragraph can often be corrected directly. A fake citation creates a deeper problem because it undermines accountability itself.

When researchers encounter a questionable claim, they normally follow the reference trail to inspect the original evidence. Hallucinated citations break that process. Readers may spend significant time searching for papers that do not exist, attempting to locate inaccessible sources or trying to reconcile contradictory publication details. This diverts effort away from actual research and slows knowledge verification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12658395/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">by J Linardon · 2025 · Cited by 31 — Fabricated references can mislead readers, distort scientific understanding, and compromise the i...</span></span></span>

Fabricated references also distort scholarly credit. Citations influence professional reputation, hiring decisions, grant evaluations and measures of research impact. When AI systems invent sources or misattribute work, they interfere with the mechanisms used to recognise intellectual contributions. Emerging evidence suggests that citation [hallucinations]({{ 'hallucinations/' | relative_url }}) may even reinforce existing inequalities by disproportionately assigning apparent authority to already prominent researchers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07723" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">LLM hallucinations in the wild: Large-scale evidence from non-existent citationsMay 8, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 8, 2026</span></span></span>

Another concern is that citations often function as evidence in themselves. Literature reviews, systematic reviews and policy reports depend heavily on accurate reference lists. If the references are fabricated, then conclusions built upon them may appear supported when no supporting evidence actually exists. Some scholars have argued that hallucinated citations can become a form of research misconduct when references serve as substantive data within an academic argument.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/08989621.2026.2645390" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tandfonline.com">[Taylor &amp; Francis Online]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tandfonline.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Taylor &amp; Francis OnlineHallucinated citations produced by generative artificial...by DB Resnik · 2026 · Cited by 2 — Hallucinated citati...</span></span></span>

## Why Fake References Scale Across Papers

The most serious risk is not a single false citation but the possibility of citation pollution.

Research literature operates as a network. Authors cite previous authors, reviewers evaluate reference lists selectively, and later researchers often reuse citations from earlier work. Once a fabricated reference enters that network, it can spread. A later author may assume the citation was checked previously and repeat it without independent verification. ## What This Means for Critical Thinking

The rise of hallucinated references changes what it means to evaluate evidence. Traditionally, readers could often treat a properly formatted citation as a reasonable indication that a source existed. Increasingly, that assumption is unsafe.

Critical thinking now requires distinguishing between the appearance of documentation and actual documentation. A citation should be treated as the beginning of verification, not the end of it. Researchers, students and readers must increasingly ask practical questions: Does the paper exist? Does the citation lead to the claimed source? Does the source support the argument being made? Is the publication information accurate?

The broader lesson is that fluent presentation and scholarly formatting are not substitutes for evidence. AI systems can reproduce the outward signals of academic credibility with remarkable accuracy while failing to provide the underlying foundation that gives those signals meaning. In research, the most damaging hallucinations are often not the obviously absurd ones.
