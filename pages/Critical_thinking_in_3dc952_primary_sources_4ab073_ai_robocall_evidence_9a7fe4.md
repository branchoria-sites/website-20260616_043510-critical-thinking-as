## Introduction

The New Hampshire AI robocall case became one of the first major tests of how authorities could trace, document and penalise AI-generated election misinformation. What made the episode especially important for [critical thinking]({{ 'critical-thinking-in-the-age-of-social/' | relative_url }}) was not simply that a cloned voice appeared to mimic President Joe Biden. It was that official investigations gradually reconstructed a source trail: who allegedly commissioned the call, how it was distributed, which laws were invoked, and how proposed penalties evolved into later enforcement actions. The case shows why election misinformation claims should be checked against [official records]({{ 'official-records/' | relative_url }}) rather than headlines alone. As new facts emerged, the story changed from a general warning about AI deepfakes into a detailed evidence trail involving telecommunications records, caller-ID spoofing, regulatory enforcement and criminal proceedings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://apnews.com/article/f3469ceb6dd613079092287994663db5" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: apnews.com">[AP News+2doj.nh.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">apnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The call, sent to multiple voters, falsely claimed that voting in the primary would support Republicans and urged voters to save their vo...</span></span></span>

## How An AI Robocall Became An Evidence Trail

On 21 January 2024, thousands of New Hampshire voters reportedly received an automated call that sounded like President Biden and urged recipients to “save” their votes for the November election rather than participate in the Democratic primary. The call also used Biden’s familiar phrase, “What a bunch of malarkey”, helping create the impression that it came from him. Investigators immediately treated the message as a potential voter-suppression effort and warned voters to disregard it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://apnews.com/article/f3469ceb6dd613079092287994663db5" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: apnews.com">[AP News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">apnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The call, sent to multiple voters, falsely claimed that voting in the primary would support Republicans and urged voters to save their vo...</span></span></span>

The early public story focused on the existence of a convincing AI-generated voice. The official investigations added a different question: how did the call travel from creation to delivery?

Authorities eventually assembled a chain that included:

* The creation of an AI-generated voice clone resembling Biden.
* The use of spoofed caller-ID information.
* Telecommunications providers that transmitted the calls.
* Individuals and companies allegedly involved in commissioning or distributing the message.
* Records linking the campaign to specific dates immediately before the primary election.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-402762A1.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: docs.fcc.gov">[FCC Docs+2doj.nh.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">docs.fcc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FCC Docsfcc proposes $6 million fine for illegal robocalls that used...23 May 2024 — Two days before the New Hampshire 2024 presidential...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2024</span></span></span>

By May 2024, New Hampshire Attorney General John Formella announced criminal charges against political consultant Steven Kramer, alleging that he had orchestrated the robocall campaign. The state indictment accused Kramer of voter suppression and candidate impersonation offences connected to the AI-generated calls.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.doj.nh.gov/news-and-media/steven-kramer-charged-voter-suppression-over-ai-generated-president-biden-robocalls" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: doj.nh.gov">[doj.nh.gov+2AP News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">doj.nh.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Formella announces that today, Steven Kramer, age 54, of New Orleans, LA, has been indicted on...Read more...</span></span></span>

The Federal Communications Commission (FCC) simultaneously released detailed findings describing how the robocalls used AI-generated voice-cloning technology and inaccurate caller-ID information. Those findings transformed the incident from a rumour about a deepfake into a documented enforcement case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-402762A1.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: docs.fcc.gov">[FCC Docs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">docs.fcc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FCC Docsfcc proposes $6 million fine for illegal robocalls that used...23 May 2024 — Two days before the New Hampshire 2024 presidential...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2024</span></span></span>

## What the Official Investigation Added to Headlines

Many headlines reduced the incident to “fake [Biden robocalls]({{ 'biden-robocall/' | relative_url }})”. The official records supplied details that significantly changed the public understanding of the event.

First, investigators identified a specific timeline. The calls were placed two days before the New Hampshire primary, making timing a central part of the alleged election-interference theory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-402762A1.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: docs.fcc.gov">[FCC Docs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">docs.fcc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FCC Docsfcc proposes $6 million fine for illegal robocalls that used...23 May 2024 — Two days before the New Hampshire 2024 presidential...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2024</span></span></span>

Second, investigators traced the telecommunications path. FCC documents focused not only on the content of the message but also on the transmission process, including caller-ID spoofing and the role of telecom providers that carried the calls. This shifted attention from the AI voice itself to the infrastructure that enabled distribution.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-402762A1.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: docs.fcc.gov">[FCC Docs+2FCC Docs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">docs.fcc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FCC Docsfcc proposes $6 million fine for illegal robocalls that used...23 May 2024 — Two days before the New Hampshire 2024 presidential...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2024</span></span></span>

Third, official findings identified multiple potentially liable actors. The alleged organiser, the transmitting telecom company and related service providers all became part of the enforcement story. The [source trail]({{ 'source-trail/' | relative_url }}) therefore extended beyond the creator of the synthetic voice.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-404951A1.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: docs.fcc.gov">[FCC Docs+2Reuters]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">docs.fcc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FCC DocsLingo Telecom to Pay $1 Million Civil...Aug 21, 2024 — FCC SETTLES CASE AGAINST PROVIDER THAT TRANSMITTED. Finally, the investigation highlighted a common misunderstanding about AI election misinformation. The most legally significant issue was not necessarily that AI was used. Regulators repeatedly focused on deceptive caller identification, robocall rules and election-related conduct. The AI voice became important because it allegedly increased the credibility of a misleading message.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-402762A1.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: docs.fcc.gov">[FCC Docs+2FCC Docs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">docs.fcc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FCC Docsfcc proposes $6 million fine for illegal robocalls that used...23 May 2024 — Two days before the New Hampshire 2024 presidential...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2024</span></span></span>

## How Proposed Fines Differ From Final Penalties

One reason primary documents matter is that proposed enforcement actions are often reported as if they are final outcomes.

In May 2024, the FCC proposed a $6 million penalty against Steven Kramer. The agency's Notice of Apparent Liability stated that the fine was proposed and subject to the FCC's enforcement process. At that stage, the figure represented an allegation and proposed sanction, not a completed punishment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-24-59A1.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: docs.fcc.gov">[FCC Docs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">docs.fcc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FCC DocsFederal Communications Commission FCC 24-59 1May 24, 2024 — We propose a penalty of $6,000,000 against Steve Kramer (Kramer) for...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 24, 2024</span></span></span>

Later, in September 2024, the FCC issued a final order imposing the $6 million fine. Headlines that only referenced the earlier proposal missed the distinction between an accusation and a completed regulatory action.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-405811A1.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: docs.fcc.gov">[FCC Docs+2fcc.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">docs.fcc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FCC Docsfcc fines man behind election interference scheme $6...26 Sept 2024 — a $6 million fine against political consultant Steve Krame...</span></span></span>

A similar shift occurred with Lingo Telecom, the company that transmitted the calls. Earlier enforcement discussions referenced a proposed $2 million penalty. The eventual settlement required a $1 million payment and compliance measures instead. Someone relying on an early report could easily repeat a figure that no longer reflected the official outcome.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://apnews.com/article/9e9cc63a71eb9c78b9bb0d1ec2aa6e9c" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: apnews.com">[Reuters+3AP News+3FCC Docs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">apnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The FCC proposed the fine, marking the first case involving generative AI technology, and also fined Lingo Telecom $2 million for transmi...</span></span></span>

This distinction illustrates a broader critical-thinking lesson: regulatory announcements often move through stages such as investigation, proposed penalties, settlements and final orders. Treating these stages as identical can produce inaccurate claims.
