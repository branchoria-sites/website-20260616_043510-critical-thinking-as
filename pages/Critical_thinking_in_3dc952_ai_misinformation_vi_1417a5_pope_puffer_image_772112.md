## Introduction

In March 2023, an image of Pope Francis wearing an oversized white puffer jacket spread rapidly across social media, including X, where many users assumed it was a genuine photograph. It was not. The image had been generated using the AI image tool Midjourney, yet it looked convincing enough to fool journalists, celebrities and ordinary users alike. The episode became one of the first major public demonstrations of how modern generative AI could create highly believable visual fiction at almost no cost.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.reuters.com/article/fact-check/image-of-pope-francis-wearing-oversized-white-puffer-coat-is-ai-generated-idUSL1N36120G/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: reuters.com">[Reuters+2CBS News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">reuters.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Image of Pope Francis wearing oversized white puffer coat...29 Mar 2023 — An image showing Pope Francis wearing a white-colored p...</span></span></span>

## The fake puffer jacket that tested everyone

The viral image appeared online in late March 2023 and depicted Pope Francis in a dramatic white coat resembling luxury streetwear. The combination was unexpected but not entirely implausible. Francis is frequently photographed in white clothing, and the image retained enough familiar details—his face, posture, cross and surroundings—to feel authentic at first glance. The picture was later confirmed to be AI-generated using Midjourney.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.reuters.com/article/fact-check/image-of-pope-francis-wearing-oversized-white-puffer-coat-is-ai-generated-idUSL1N36120G/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: reuters.com">[Reuters+2CBS News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">reuters.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Image of Pope Francis wearing oversized white puffer coat...29 Mar 2023 — An image showing Pope Francis wearing a white-colored p...</span></span></span>

The image's creator reportedly intended it as a humorous experiment rather than a deception campaign. Yet once the picture escaped its original context, viewers encountered it as a standalone image detached from information about its creation. This is a common pattern in social media environments: content is shared faster than context.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://gijn.org/resource/guide-detecting-ai-generated-content/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gijn.org">[GIJN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gijn.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">guide detecting ai generated content</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Reporter&#x27;s Guide to Detecting AI-Generated Content1 Sept 2025 — This viral image of Pope Francis I in a white Balenciaga puffer coat...</span></span></span>

The incident quickly became a reference point in discussions about [synthetic media]({{ 'synthetic-media/' | relative_url }}). Fact-checking organisations published explanations, while news outlets used the image to illustrate how rapidly AI-generated visuals had improved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://fullfact.org/news/puffer-coat-pope-ai-generated/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fullfact.org">[Full Fact+2Reuters]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fullfact.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">puffer coat pope ai generated</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Full FactViral pictures of Pope Francis wearing puffer jacket are AI-...27 Mar 2023 — While these images look extremely realistic, they...</span></span></span>

## Why the image was so shareable

The Pope puffer image succeeded because it occupied a narrow space between the absurd and the believable.

If the Pope had been shown flying through space or riding a dragon, most viewers would have recognised it as fiction immediately. If he had been shown in ordinary religious clothing, the image would have been unremarkable. The puffer jacket sat in a sweet spot: unusual enough to be amusing, but plausible enough to trigger uncertainty.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gq.com/story/pope-puffer-jacket-midjourney-ai-meme" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gq.com">[GQ]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gq.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Pope Francis Puffer Photo Was Real in Our Hearts</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Many believed the image to be real due to the Pope&#x27;s practical wardrobe choices. In such cases, misinformation can spread even when belief is uncertain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.poynter.org/fact-checking/2023/pope-francis-balenciaga-puffer-jacket-artificial-intelligence-fact-check/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: poynter.org">[Poynter]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">poynter.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">pope francis balenciaga puffer jacket artificial intelligence fact check</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">It was actually created with the AI image...Read more...</span></span></span>

## Why casual fakes still matter

One misconception about [AI misinformation]({{ 'ai-virality/' | relative_url }}) is that only malicious or political deception deserves attention. The Pope puffer image suggests otherwise.

The image caused little direct harm on its own. Yet it demonstrated that highly realistic synthetic imagery could achieve mass visibility before most viewers questioned its authenticity. That matters because public habits formed around harmless examples can later influence reactions to more consequential ones. reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk+2The Guardian<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/news/will-ai-generated-images-create-new-crisis-fact-checkers-experts-are-not-so-sure" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk">[reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">will ai generated images create new crisis fact checkers experts are not so sure</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Will AI-generated images create a new crisis for fact-...11 Apr 2023 — The rise of AI generated images is indeed a growing concern since...</span></span></span>

Researchers and media analysts have argued that the significance of the image lay less in its content than in what it revealed about audience behaviour. Millions of users encountered a fabricated photograph that looked credible enough to bypass normal scepticism. The lesson was not merely that AI could generate convincing pictures, but that people often trust photographs by default.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://time.com/6266606/how-to-spot-deepfake-pope/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Pause before sharing.">[Time+2reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Pause before sharing.</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on time.com.</span></span></span>

The episode also highlighted a growing tension in digital culture. Historically, photographs carried an assumption of evidential value. While manipulated images have existed for decades, modern generative AI allows realistic scenes to be created from scratch rather than altered from existing photographs. ## Visual checks before reposting

The Pope puffer image also became a useful teaching example because subtle clues existed, even if many viewers missed them.

Some analysts pointed to unusual details in the cross necklace, clothing folds and other small inconsistencies that did not behave exactly as real-world objects should. These imperfections were easier to spot after careful inspection than during fast scrolling.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://time.com/6266606/how-to-spot-deepfake-pope/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Pause before sharing.">[Time]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Pause before sharing.</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on time.com.</span></span></span>

When encountering a surprising image on social media, especially one that seems designed to provoke amazement or amusement, several checks can help:

1. **Look for independent reporting.** If a major public figure has appeared somewhere unusual, multiple reputable news organisations will often have photographs from the same event.
2. **Inspect fine details.** Jewellery, hands, text, reflections and object interactions frequently reveal inconsistencies in AI-generated images.
3. **Check the source.** Trace the image back to its earliest known posting rather than relying on reposts.
4. **Use reverse-image searches.** These can reveal [fact-checks]({{ 'fact-checks/' | relative_url }}), earlier versions or discussions about authenticity.

5. **Pause before sharing.** Viral images often rely on immediate emotional reactions that discourage verification. [Time+2Le Monde.fr](https://time.com/6266606/how-to-spot-deepfake-pope/)

The deeper lesson is not that every striking image is fake. It is that visual realism is no longer sufficient [evidence]({{ 'evidence/' | relative_url }}) that an event occurred. The Pope puffer image became memorable precisely because it exposed how easily modern AI can exploit our instinct to trust what appears photographic.
